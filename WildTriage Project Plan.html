Feasible in 8 weeks for 2 people, but only with a focused scope. R

What to build: a self-hosted camera-trap triage and verification platform (working name WildTriage). It turns raw camera-trap images and videos into verified species records. Its main claim is measured: how much human review it saves at a controlled error rate.

3.2 The chosen contribution (A)

Problem. A camera-trap deployment produces a stream of images. At each interval, an ecologist can review only a budget b of items. All other predictions are either auto-accepted or left unverified.

What we propose and evaluate R. A triage loop with four parts: 1. Calibrated confidence. Temperature scaling, fitted only on labels already reviewed at that site. 2. Risk-controlled auto-acceptance. - Split-conformal / conformal-risk-control thresholds that target error ≤ α among auto-accepted items. - Adaptive conformal inference (ACI) variants that track α online under temporal shift. - Conditioning by day vs night/IR ("Mondrian" groups), which ties to the lighting requirement. 3. Budgeted review selection. Uncertainty × class-balance quotas: review the items most likely to be wrong, with minority species protected. 4. Site adaptation from verified labels. StreamTrap recipe: Balanced Softmax with a linear head (or LoRA on BioCLIP 2), plus WiSE interpolation to protect zero-shot behaviour.

What is and is not new [R, after the related-work search in §3.3]. Every ingredient is existing work: temperature scaling, conformal risk control, Learn-then-Test, adaptive conformal inference, uncertainty-based and class-balanced selection, Balanced Softmax, WiSE. We do not claim a new method. What we propose is the combination, evaluated under realistic conditions: - chronological streams at many real camera sites (StreamTrap-style), not a single i.i.d. split; - a fixed review budget that is split between certification (random labels) and learning (selected labels); - whether the auto-accept error target holds on future intervals, with day and night treated separately; - how many selected labels close the gap to the all-labels oracle that StreamTrap uses.

The honest framing is an empirical study with negative results allowed. Whether it is publishable depends on results and on the literature check below.

Novelty status U. In about ten searches I found no camera-trap paper that sets review thresholds with a finite-sample guarantee (conformal risk control or Learn-then-Test). Absence from a few searches is not proof; W1 repeats the search properly (arXiv, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, ecology journals) and the claim stays unmade until then.

Feasibility. BioCLIP 2 embeddings for about 200k crops take minutes to roughly an hour on the RTX 4000 Ada A. Linear heads take seconds and LoRA takes hours. Every policy is simulated over the cached predictions.

Data: "StreamTrap-mini". Rebuilt from LILA following the StreamTrap protocol: - Sites with ≥6 months and ≥1,000 animal images. - Crops from LILA's precomputed MegaDetector results (V MDv5a/b, MDv1000-redwood, and repeat-detection-eliminated versions), with boxes enlarged by 50%. - Chronological monthly intervals. - Target 30 sites: 10 dev and 20 test, from ≥3 LILA datasets.

Design input from the simulator (synthetic data, checks the mechanics only; not a result). research/experiments/exp1_triage.py --synthetic shows that the size of the random calibration slice decides whether anything can be auto-accepted. - With α = 5% and δ = 0.1, Learn-then-Test needs at least ln δ / ln(1 − α) ≈ 45 error-free accepted calibration labels per group before it can certify a threshold. - Splitting calibration by day/night therefore costs coverage: 4% vs 12% in the smoke run. - Exp 1 must therefore vary the calibration fraction ({0.2, 0.3, 0.5}). It must also test pooling calibration across sites against per-site calibration (pooling assumes sites are exchangeable).

Academic value: a clear empirical study with guarantees-vs-reality analysis and negative results allowed.

Commercial value: the headline product metric, "% of images auto-accepted at ≤α error" and "labels needed per site".

Secondary (P0 reproduction, P1 analysis): - Exp 2: SA-FARI video counting. SAM 3 vs MegaDetector + ByteTrack per-clip MaxN error, by subset (night, small, multi-animal, challenging). - Exp 2b (P1): clip-level routing (direction B). - Exp 3 (P1): DepthSAM reproduction plus hard-crop refinement. - Exp 4 (P1): aerial counting with OWL/HerdNet.

4. Product specification

Target users: - conservation NGOs and protected-area teams running camera-trap grids; - university ecology labs; - environmental consultancies doing biodiversity monitoring for impact assessments [A: market hypothesis].

Problem. Manual review of camera-trap media is the bottleneck. Generic AI labels have unknown, site-dependent error and give no indication of which labels to trust.

Core workflows: 1. Create a project with a region and species list. 2. Register deployments (sites/cameras and their dates). 3. Upload a card dump (folder or zip of images/videos). 4. Processing runs asynchronously with live progress. 5. The system triages each item: auto-accept, review queue or empty. 6. Reviewers verify the queue by keyboard. 7. (P1) Adapt the site model from verified labels and re-triage. 8. Analytics and export (CSV, COCO, Camtrap DP P1).

Inference pipeline (per media item)

  1. Validate. Check magic bytes; read metadata (EXIF, ffprobe); compute SHA-256 to catch duplicates; compute quality features: - night/IR flag: low chroma, mean |R−G|+|G−B| below a threshold; - blur: variance of the Laplacian; - brightness.
  2. Image path. - Run MegaDetector (confidence ≥ τ_det). - Crop each detection with the box enlarged 50%. - Run BioCLIP 2 against the project's species prompts, or the site head if one exists. - Apply temperature scaling. - Aggregate over the camera's burst/sequence (LILA's seq_id or a time gap under 30 s).
  3. Video path. - Run MegaDetector on frames sampled at 1 fps. If there is no animal, mark the clip as an empty candidate (this saves GPU). - Otherwise run SAM 3 at 6 fps (matches SA-FARI) with the prompt "animal" plus the top-k species names from the classifier. - Output masklets → tracks → per-track species vote → MaxN (the most animals visible in any one frame).
  4. Triage policy (versioned). Outputs: - auto_accept / review(priority, reasons[]) / auto_empty; - α and the budget come from project settings.
  5. Review writes verification_decisions, which are append-only and audited.
  6. Adaptation (P1 in-app; P0 in research) creates a new model_version from verified labels, then re-triages.

Dependency isolation R: - SAM 3 needs Python ≥3.12 and PyTorch ≥2.7 / CUDA ≥12.6 V. - DepthSAM ships old SAM-v1 code with unpinned requirements V. - So DepthSAM gets its own virtual environment (or image), and a worker started only for gpu-heavy camouflage jobs. - If MegaDetector's dependencies conflict, it gets its own environment too. Each job row names its queue, and each worker claims only its own queues.

VRAM policy (20 GB lab PC, 4 GB T1000) R: - Use bf16 where the GPU supports it (Ada); fall back to fp16 (Turing T1000, which has no bf16) or fp32 on CPU. wildtriage_ml.registry.pick_dtype picks automatically. - Each GPU worker's ModelRegistry has a VRAM budget (18 GB on the lab PC, 3.5 GB on the T1000). It evicts the least-recently-used model before loading another, and refuses models that can never fit. - What fits on the T1000 [A: measure in W1]: MegaDetector (about 1.5 GB) and BioCLIP 2 ViT-L/14 in fp16 (about 2 GB, small batches), one at a time if needed. SAM 3, DepthSAM and VLMs run on the lab PC only (gpu-heavy). - Small models stay resident: MegaDetector and BioCLIP 2 together are about 3 GB A. - SAM 3 loads on demand. - Run DepthSAM and any VLM only in their own job batches, never resident at the same time as SAM 3. - Split long videos into chunks of ≤ 30 s at 6 fps. - Log torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated() on every job, so the efficiency table comes for free.

Why a Postgres queue instead of Celery + Redis R: - Redis has no official Windows build, and the lab PC may not allow Docker or WSL2. - Celery's Windows support is limited. - A jobs table claimed with SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED removes a service. It also makes "create job + enqueue" a single transaction, and works natively and in Docker alike. - Volume is low: one GPU worker, thousands of jobs rather than millions. - If you later need more scale, the same interface can be backed by a Postgres task-queue library such as Procrastinate, or by Celery.

9. Research and evaluation plan

Research questions: - RQ1 (calibration under drift). Does post-hoc calibration fitted on past intervals stay calibrated on future intervals, per site and for day vs night? - RQ2 (risk control). Does conformal / ACI auto-acceptance keep realized error ≤ α on future intervals, and at what coverage? - RQ3 (label efficiency). At a fixed review budget, how close do uncertainty × class-balanced selection plus the StreamTrap recipe get to oracle accuracy? - RQ4 (video counting). Does SAM 3 beat MegaDetector + ByteTrack at per-clip counts, and on which SA-FARI subsets?

Hypotheses (to test, not claims): - H1: temperature scaling lowers ECE on the next interval compared with uncalibrated scores. - H2: ACI with day/night groups has fewer α-violations than split-conformal across test sites. - H3: at a 10% budget, our policy gives higher final-label macro-F1 than random or least-confidence review (paired bootstrap over sites, p < 0.05). - H4: SAM 3 has lower MaxN error than MegaDetector + ByteTrack, overall and on night clips.

Baselines: - zero-shot BioCLIP 2 with no review; - a raw-softmax threshold; - random review, least-confidence, margin, entropy and k-center review at the same budget; - naive fine-tuning; - StreamTrap's "accumulated with recipe" (all labels; the oracle); - SpeciesNet (reference; possible leakage); - for video: MegaDetector + ByteTrack / OC-SORT (SA-FARI rows).

Cloud fallback for GPU-heavy batch jobs (molab)

What was found [V from marimo's announcement, blog and newsletter 25; one search, not cross-checked against molab's docs]: - molab is marimo's cloud notebook service. It is free in public preview and runs on CoreWeave. - Default notebook: 4 CPUs, 32 GB RAM. An opt-in GPU is available: an RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell with 96 GB VRAM. marimo says GPUs are free "as long as usage is reasonable" and does not define that. - Sessions last at most 12 hours. Notebooks idle for more than 90 minutes are shut down automatically.

What to run there R: - SAM 3 runs on the SA-FARI test set (Exp 2). - DepthSAM reproduction (Exp 3). - BioCLIP 2 embedding extraction for Exp 1.

What not to run there R: - The app and the live demo (it needs a server that stays up). - Anything that cannot be stopped and resumed.

Rules that make the fallback safe: 1. Chunked, resumable jobs. Every experiment script writes its results after each chunk (for example per site, per clip batch, or per 500 images) and skips chunks that already exist. A 12-hour limit or an idle shutdown then costs minutes, not days. 2. Durable output. Save embeddings, predictions and metrics somewhere that outlives the session (the team drive or a git-ignored results folder you copy out). [U: does molab keep files between sessions? Test in W1.] 3. No secrets in notebooks. Use the token mechanism molab provides for the Hugging Face token (SAM 3, SA-FARI are gated). Never paste it into a cell or commit it. [U: how molab stores secrets.] 4. Pinned environment. Run uv pip install -e ml plus pinned versions from the repo, so results match the lab PC. Record the molab GPU name and package versions in each evaluation_runs row. 5. Same code path. Notebooks only call research/experiments/*.py and wildtriage_ml, so there is one implementation to test. 6. Licenses still apply. SA-FARI and DepthSAM's depth model are non-commercial: fine for coursework. Do not upload anything the dataset terms forbid redistributing.

Preview risks: limits, quotas or pricing can change, "reasonable usage" is undefined, and a shared GPU may be unavailable at peak times. Treat molab as the first fallback and Colab or Kaggle (free tiers with limits) as the second. If neither works, scale down: fewer SA-FARI test clips (a fixed, documented subset) and SAM 3 at 3 fps.
