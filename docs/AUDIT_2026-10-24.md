## 3. Research and publication assessment

### 3.1 Where it stands

| Level | Where WildTriage is | What gets it to the next level |
|---|---|---|
| Course-level implementation | **Reachable by 13 Dec** if Weeks 2–5 run the three papers' models on real data | Real models, SA-FARI subset reproduction, DepthSAM reproduction, Exp 1 on real sites |
| Strong portfolio project | **Reachable by 13 Dec** | The above, plus a public repo, a demo video, a clean write-up, and evidence each member wrote substantial parts |
| Potentially publishable contribution | **Not yet.** A design and a synthetic simulator only. | Real multi-site results that show something non-obvious, with baselines and confidence intervals |
| Publication-ready submission | **Not before Feb–Mar 2027** | A larger evaluation, a clear contribution, related work done properly, a paper draft reviewed by a supervisor |

### 3.2 Main weaknesses [R]

1. **No empirical evidence yet.** The only results are synthetic, and there our strategy did not beat least-confidence review.
2. **Contribution risk.** Every component is existing work (plan §3.3). A combination study is publishable only if it reveals something reviewers didn't know. Examples: how often error guarantees fail across real camera sites, or how a review budget should be split between certifying and learning.
3. **Scale.** About 30 sites is small next to StreamTrap's 546. Reviewers will ask why.
4. **Human factor.** A "mini-study" with 2–4 reviewers is too weak for claims about review time. Use a simulated reviewer cost, and present the human study as a sanity check only.
5. **Literature check.** About 10 searches is not a literature review.

### 3.3 Recommended research reframing [R]

Lead with the question the simulator already pointed at, which is less crowded than "our selection beats theirs":

> **RQ\*: Under temporal shift at real camera sites, how should a fixed human-review budget be split between *certifying* automatic acceptance (random labels for a statistical guarantee) and *learning* (selected labels for site adaptation), and how often do the guarantees fail?**

**Why this framing:**
- It turns the simulator's finding into the centre of the paper. Certifying a 5% error needs at least about 45 error-free calibration labels per group (plan §3.2).
- It builds directly on StreamTrap's stated open problem: when to adapt.
- Its deliverable is measurable: a curve of budget split against final-label accuracy and guarantee-violation rate, per site.
- It holds up even if no selection strategy wins.

**What the existing plan keeps:** the baselines, metrics, the calibration fraction ablation and the guarantee-violation analysis. They become the core of the paper rather than ablations.

### 3.4 What a reviewer will want [R]

| Need | Minimum for a workshop paper | For a main-conference paper |
|---|---|---|
| Sites | ≥30 sites from ≥3 LILA datasets | ≥100 sites, or the full StreamTrap set once it is released |
| Baselines | Zero-shot; fixed-threshold routing (Huebner-style); least-confidence; StreamTrap mean-max-softmax adapt-or-skip; the all-labels oracle | Plus active-learning baselines (Norouzzadeh-style) and conformal-set baselines (Fermanian et al.) |
| Statistics | 3 seeds; bootstrap confidence intervals over sites; paired tests | Plus a sensitivity analysis for every parameter you tuned |
| Generality | 1 classifier (BioCLIP 2) | ≥2 backbones (BioCLIP 2 + SpeciesNet features) |
| Reproducibility | Code + manifests public | Plus a released benchmark split |
| Novelty | Clear framing + negative results | A method contribution: e.g. a budget-split rule with a guarantee, or a sequence-aware certification |

## 10. Final recommendation

**Spend the next two weeks on the parts that cannot be faked:** real models on real data for all three papers, and Exp 1 on real camera sites. The platform is already ahead. Its value now depends on showing real predictions and real triage numbers.

1. **Monday 26 Oct (M0).** Clear every unblocker in §0 (access, IT, instructor, rubric, AI policy, funding), move the repo into git, and add Mac support. These are cheap, and every later milestone depends on them.
2. **By 1 Nov (M1).** See real MegaDetector and BioCLIP 2 predictions in the review UI, and SAM 3 on three SA-FARI clips. If that slips past 8 Nov, the cut list applies early.
3. **Reframe the research** around how a fixed review budget should be split between certifying and learning, and how often the guarantees fail at real sites. It survives a null result, it is honest about novelty, and it builds directly on StreamTrap's open question.
4. **Keep all three papers in the deliverable** by making a minimal DepthSAM reproduction P0.
5. **Write the course report as a workshop paper** from the start. Plan a zero-cost arXiv preprint in early 2027. Submit to a CVPR 2027 workshop or an ecology journal only if someone else covers the fees.
6. **Make the work yours.** Each member should author and be able to explain the real-model integration, the experiments and the UI work. That matters for the grade, for integrity, and for every interview where this project comes up.

If those six things happen, the realistic result by 13 December is:
- an excellent course project;
- a portfolio piece that shows research, engineering and product sense together;
- a credible path to a workshop paper or journal article in spring 2027.

The project is not a business yet, and that is fine for this timeframe.
